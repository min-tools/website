Build a lightweight macOS clipboard history app that helps users quickly find and reuse everything they copy. Keep the experience native, fast, and privacy-focused, with a clean interface and powerful search.

Everything you copied

Copy as usual. Find it later.

Pastemin watches the clipboard while it runs and keeps what you copy in its own sandboxed folder. When you need something back, one shortcut opens the history, and Return puts it on the clipboard again.

Find an earlier copy in seconds

Press ⌃⌥C, start typing, and search the complete text of your clipboard history. Results update while you type, and the newest copies come first.

Text and images together

Preview copied text at full length and inspect images without opening another app. Each item shows the app it came from and when.

Made for the keyboard

Move with the arrow keys, press Return to restore an item, delete one with ⌘⌫, and dismiss with Escape. The search field is ready the moment the panel opens.

Optional automatic paste

Pastemin can send one ⌘V after you choose an item. It is off by default and works only after you turn it on and grant macOS permission. It never reads your keystrokes.

Your history stays on this Mac

Clipboard content lives inside Pastemin's sandbox. There is no Pastemin account, no analytics, no advertising, and no developer server.

You decide what remains

Keep copied items for an hour, a day, a week, a month, three months, or forever. Clear one item, the current clipboard, or the whole history whenever you want.

Sensitive items are respected

Pastemin skips entries marked concealed, temporary, or automatically generated. Password managers use these markers to keep sensitive items out of clipboard history.

At home on macOS

Use the menu bar, choose your own global shortcut, and work in any of thirty-one interface languages. Native AppKit and SwiftUI, on Apple silicon.

Fast with a long history

Large text and image payloads load only when needed, so a long history does not have to enter memory at launch. The list renders in batches as you scroll.

Find and restore

Type a word. Press Return.

Start typing in the search field to find matches in the full text of your accessible history. Choose an item to copy it again and return to the app you were using.

  • Full-text search

    Search the full text of accessible items, including long notes. After the trial, the free tier searches the five newest items.

  • Back to the top

    Restoring an older item copies it again and moves it to the top of the history.

  • Copy only, or paste too

    By default the item is copied and you paste it yourself. With automatic paste on and allowed, Pastemin pastes it for you.

Your clipboard, your rules

Keep it for an hour, or for good.

Choose how long copied items remain, whether Pastemin sits in the menu bar, and which shortcut opens it. Expired items and their stored files are removed automatically.

  • Retention you set

    One hour, one day, one week, one month, three months, or forever. Change it any time; the history follows.

    Delete one item with ⌘⌫, clear the current clipboard, or clear the whole history. Clearing history leaves what other apps still hold untouched.

  • See where it lives

    The storage summary shows how much space the history uses, and Show in Finder opens its local folder.

Private by design

Your clipboard history stays on your Mac.

Pastemin stores clipboard history only in its sandboxed Application Support folder. Clipboard content is never uploaded. The only network traffic is Apple's own StoreKit, for product and purchase information.

  • Marked items are never stored

    Pastemin skips items marked concealed, transient, or automatically generated. It cannot detect sensitive content that an app copies without those markers.

  • Permission only when you ask for it

    When you enable automatic paste and select an item, Pastemin asks for permission to send the paste keystroke. If you decline, the item is still copied. You can revoke access in System Settings.

  • No usage tracking

    No account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking SDKs, and no iCloud sync of your history. Apple handles purchases and never shares payment details with the developer.

The details

Small things, done carefully.

Requirements

An Apple-silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later. Normal copy and paste needs no Accessibility access. Optional automatic paste asks for permission to send the paste keystroke.

Short setup

The setup assistant sets retention, the menu bar, and the shortcut, explains automatic paste, and spells out the 30-day trial before you start.

Menu bar app

Pastemin runs in the menu bar without a Dock icon. Quit it to stop clipboard monitoring.

Automatic paste, explained

With permission, Pastemin uses macOS's public event-posting APIs to send one ⌘V after you choose an item. It does not inspect other apps' interfaces or watch the keyboard.

Sandboxed

Pastemin runs in the App Sandbox and stores history in its own container. It collects no analytics and sends no clipboard content to the developer.

Thirty-one languages

The interface is localized into thirty-one languages and follows your Mac's appearance, contrast, and accent settings.

Source available

The source is on GitHub under the PolyForm Strict License 1.0.0 with added permissions. You can inspect how Pastemin handles your clipboard.

Duplicates and limits

Copying an item again moves it to the top without creating a duplicate. Long items load their full text when you view or search them.
